# Visual Abstract Plan for Alcohol Intervention Study

## Paper Analysis
- **Title**: Effectiveness of screening and ultra-brief alcohol intervention in primary care: a pragmatic cluster randomised controlled trial
- **Authors**: Ryuhei So, Kazuya Kariyama, Shunsuke Oyamada, et al.
- **Setting**: 40 primary care clinics in Japan
- **Participants**: 1,133 outpatients aged 20-74 years with hazardous drinking
- **Intervention**: Ultra-brief intervention (Ultra-BI) vs. simplified assessment only (SAO)
- **Primary Outcome**: Alcohol consumption at 12 and 24 weeks
- **Secondary Outcome**: Readiness to change drinking habits

## Key Findings to Highlight
1. Ultra-BI did not significantly reduce alcohol consumption compared to SAO
2. Ultra-BI did improve readiness to change drinking habits
3. Bayes factor analysis supported the null hypothesis for total alcohol consumption

## Visual Abstract Design Plan
- **Layout**: Three-panel design following CDC best practices
- **Panel 1**: Study design and participants (cluster RCT, 40 clinics, 1,133 participants)
- **Panel 2**: Interventions comparison (Ultra-BI vs. SAO)
- **Panel 3**: Key findings (alcohol consumption vs. readiness to change)
- **Color Scheme**: Professional medical color palette (blues, greens)
- **Visual Elements**: Icons for clinics, participants, alcohol, and behavioral change
- **Typography**: Clear, readable fonts with hierarchical structure

- Design software: Adobe Illustrator/Photoshop or open-source alternative (GIMP, Inkscape)

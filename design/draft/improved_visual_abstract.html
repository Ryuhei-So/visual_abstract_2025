Effectiveness of screening and ultra-brief alcohol intervention in primary care:

a pragmatic cluster randomised controlled trial

❝ Summary
📊

Primary Finding

Ultra-brief intervention (Ultra-BI) did not significantly reduce alcohol consumption compared to simplified assessment only (SAO) at 24 weeks.

Difference: 27.8g/4 weeks (95% CI -149.7 to 205.4)

Bayes factor analysis: 0.08±0.25 (strongly supporting the null hypothesis)

📋 Study Design
Design Randomisation Risk of Bias
Pragmatic cluster RCT
40 primary care clinics in Japan		 Clinic-level randomization
20 clinics per arm		 No allocation concealment
Participants blinded to study hypothesis
👥 Population
Participants Inclusion Criteria Sample Size
Outpatients in primary care settings AUDIT-C score ≥5 for men
AUDIT-C score ≥4 for women		 Ultra-BI: n=531
SAO: n=602
⚖️ Intervention Comparison
Ultra-Brief Intervention (Ultra-BI)
👨‍⚕️
Components:
  • AUDIT screening
  • Brief oral advice (3-5 minutes)
  • Educational leaflet
Simplified Assessment Only (SAO)
📝
Components:
  • AUDIT-C screening only
  • No advice provided
  • No educational materials
📊 Primary Outcome
Total Alcohol Consumption at 24 Weeks

Measurement: Self-reported total alcohol consumption (grams) over the previous 4 weeks

Conclusion

Ultra-BI did not significantly reduce alcohol consumption compared to SAO at 24 weeks follow-up.

The Bayes factor analysis strongly supported the null hypothesis, suggesting that the intervention was not effective for reducing alcohol consumption in this population.

# Visual Abstract Mockup

## Overall Layout
A three-panel horizontal design following CDC best practices, with clear visual flow from left to right.

## Panel 1: Study Design & Participants
- **Title**: "Ultra-Brief Alcohol Intervention in Japanese Primary Care"
- **Study Design**: Pragmatic Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial
- **Setting**: 40 primary care clinics in Japan
- **Participants**: 1,133 outpatients with hazardous drinking (AUDIT-C ≥5 for men, ≥4 for women)
- **Visual Elements**: 
  - Icon of Japan with clinic locations
  - Icons representing participants (with gender ratio)
  - AUDIT-C screening tool visualization

## Panel 2: Interventions Comparison
- **Ultra-BI Group**: 
  - AUDIT screening + brief oral advice + alcohol reduction leaflet
  - Visual: Doctor speaking with patient (1 minute)
  - n=531 participants
- **SAO Group**: 
  - Simplified assessment only
  - Visual: Patient completing form
  - n=602 participants
- **Follow-up**: 12 and 24 weeks

## Panel 3: Key Findings
- **Primary Outcome**: Total Alcohol Consumption (TAC)
  - Bar chart showing Ultra-BI (1046.9g/4 weeks) vs SAO (1019.0g/4 weeks)
  - Text: "No significant difference (p>0.05)"
  - Bayes factor: 0.08±0.25 (strongly supporting null hypothesis)
- **Secondary Outcome**: Readiness to Change
  - Line graph showing improvement at 12 and 24 weeks
  - Effect sizes: Hedge's g 0.21 at 12 weeks, 0.16 at 24 weeks
  - Text: "Significant improvement (p<0.05)"

## Footer
- Authors: "Ryuhei So, et al."
- Citation: "medRxiv 2024.12.27.24319613"
- Conclusion: "Ultra-BI improves motivation to change but not actual alcohol consumption"
